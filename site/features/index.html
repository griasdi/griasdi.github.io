Screen sharing

Your game, with its sound, in AV1. A screen, a window, a capture card or a camera, into a call or a text channel. If one viewer’s line is slow, only they drop to 15 fps.

  • Codecs

    AV1 · H.264

    Hardware first: NVENC, VA-API, AMF, Quick Sync or VideoToolbox, whichever your machine has. If one fails mid-stream, the next takes over. Software AV1 is the last resort.

  • Picture

    480p · 720p · 1080p · 1440p, 30 fps

    1080p by default and never upscaled. The Text switch trades motion for sharp letters at 15 fps. Software encoding runs at 720p and 10 fps.

  • Bitrate

    up to 10 Mbit/s AV1, 15 Mbit/s H.264

    At 1440p. The stream starts lower and is retuned every 2 s from what the server reports: down by 30 % when the line is congested, up again once it’s calm.

  • Delay

    ≤ 150 ms playout buffer

    It follows the jitter of the last 3 s. A keyframe every 2 s, so a new viewer is in within 2 s.

  • Slow viewers

    up to 3 temporal layers

    The server drops the upper layers for the viewer who can’t keep up. Everyone else keeps 30 fps.

  • Sound

    Opus · 64 kbit/s

    Every app’s sound except griasdi’s own, so the call never echoes into your stream. Or one audio input, such as the capture card’s HDMI sound.

  • Capture

    Wayland through the screen portal and PipeWire, X11, Windows Graphics Capture, macOS ScreenCaptureKit. Capture cards and cameras with a live preview.

Voice

Crisp for a whole evening. The noise model eats your keyboard, the echo stays out, and everyone gets their own volume.

  • Codec

    Opus · 48 kHz · 32 kbit/s

    20 ms frames with forward error correction: a lost packet is rebuilt from the next one.

  • Jitter buffer

    40–200 ms, per speaker

    Starts at 60 ms, grows when someone’s line wobbles and shrinks back after 10 s of calm.

  • Noise suppression

    Light · Strong · Maximum

    RNNoise, DPDFNet-2 and DPDFNet-8, on the processor. If a model needs more than 60 % of real time, griasdi falls back to the standard one.

  • Clean-up

    Echo cancellation, automatic gain up to +15 dB and an 8-band equalizer from 100 Hz to 12.8 kHz.

  • Sending

    Speech detection by default, a loudness gate, or push-to-talk, on the backquote key unless you pick another.

  • Per person

    −10 to +15 dB

    A volume and a “Mute for me” for everyone, kept on your device for every channel.

  • Under load

    The audio thread runs at real-time priority on Windows, macOS and Linux, so a game on every core doesn’t cut you off.

Overlays and keys

Stay in your game. See who’s talking, pause your stream, mute yourself, all without switching windows.

  • Voice overlay

    Who is in your call, with a lamp that lights while someone talks. It sits above your game and never takes the focus or a click.

  • Stream overlay

    Your own share: live or paused, for how long, the quality and who watches, with buttons for pause, sound, microphone, deafen and stop.

  • Kept off stream

    On Windows and macOS 14.4 or newer the overlays stay out of your own screen share. Elsewhere they hide while you share a whole screen.

  • System-wide keys

    Push-to-talk, mute, deafen, leave, transcript, the overlays, pause and sound of your share. They work while the game has focus, on Windows, macOS, X11 and Wayland.

  • OBS

    “OBS overlay…” gives OBS a key and a page with your voice overlay, ready for a browser source.

  • Everything else

    Ctrl+K

    The command palette runs every action in the app. Cmd+K on a Mac.

Chat and files

Text channels, DMs and big files. Too big for the server? It goes from member to member, from everyone who already has it.

  • Messages

    Markdown, replies, reactions, edits, forwards, pins, polls with up to 10 options, read receipts, mentions, @here and @all. Drafts survive a restart.

  • Attachments

    ≤ 25 MiB, kept 1, 7 or 30 days

    Stored encrypted on the server for as long as the sender picks, from the server’s list.

  • Big files and folders

    8 GiB, or up to 1 TiB if the server allows

    Sent in 1 MiB pieces, each one checked, from everyone online who has them. If nobody is, the download waits and carries on by itself. From a folder you take only what you need.

  • Video

    Plays in the chat while it is still arriving. A YouTube player is built in.

Encryption

No accounts. Just keys. Everything is encrypted on your device. The server orders, stores and forwards what it can’t read.

  • Text

    MLS, RFC 9420

    X25519, AES-128-GCM and Ed25519. Messages are sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 and padded, so their length gives little away. New keys with every change of members.

  • Voice and screens

    SFrame, RFC 9605 · AES-128-GCM

    Keys from MLS, one per sender, replaced with every change of members.

  • Files

    A random key per file, every piece checked with BLAKE3.

  • You

    An Ed25519 key, optionally behind a passphrase. Each device has its own key, signed by yours. Join with a link: no e-mail, no phone number, no password.

  • The server sees

    Ciphertext, sizes, timing, who belongs to which group and who is muted. The names of communities, channels and roles are sealed. What a server sees

On-device models

Smart where you sit. Every model runs on your computer, on griasdi’s own inference engine. Nothing you say is sent anywhere to be analysed.

  • Noise suppression

    RNNoise · DPDFNet-2 · DPDFNet-8

    On the processor, in real time.

  • Speech detection

    A small voice model opens your microphone when you speak, not when you type.

  • Live transcripts

    NVIDIA Nemotron ASR Streaming 0.6B

    Opt in, on your graphics card (Vulkan, Metal or DirectX 12). Also dictates into the composer. Call transcripts vanish when the call ends.

  • Spoken cues

    Supertonic 3 · ten voices

    Opt in: says who joined, by name.

  • Writing check

    EuroLLM 1.7B

    Marks likely typos in English and German as you write. It never rewrites anything.

  • The engine

    griasdi’s own GPU kernels on wgpu, and a CPU path. Model files are signed and come through the server, never from a third party.

For developers

Hackable from your side. A local API for your Stream Deck and scripts, an MCP server for your agents, and bots in Python or TypeScript.

  • Local access

    127.0.0.1:13337

    Off until you turn it on in Settings, Integrations. Every program gets its own key and only the permissions you grant. Requests from web pages and other host names are refused.

  • Events and commands

    State as server-sent events from GET /v1/events; POST /v1/commands/<name> to mute, deafen, push to talk, leave, pause or stop your share and toggle the overlays.

  • MCP

    /mcp · 12 tools

    Let an agent list conversations, read and send messages, read the live transcript, and see or control your call and your share.

  • Bots

    A bot is a member with its own key. Slash commands, cards, polls, voice in 48 kHz frames, and a video file streamed as a share. Read the bot guide

  • Devices

    griasdi link ssh desktop brings a laptop in from a terminal: your desktop signs its key.

Where it runs

One app on three systems. Native, drawn on your graphics card, and it updates itself without dropping your call.

  • Linux

    x86_64 · ARM64

    AppImage or tarball. glibc 2.39 and PipeWire 0.3.53 or newer.

  • Windows

    x86_64

    Setup or portable zip. Sharing a stream’s sound needs Windows 11 or Windows 10 build 20348.

  • macOS

    Apple Silicon · macOS 14

    A .dmg.

  • Updates

    Signed builds, every piece checked. The restart keeps your place, your call and your screen share.

  • Your own server

    griasdi-server for Linux: one program, UDP and TCP port 1337, no database to run. Read the server guide

Not yet

What griasdi doesn’t do today, so you know before your first stream.

  • Over 30 fps

    Screen sharing tops out at 30 fps. No 60 fps yet.

  • Who sent a frame

    Everyone in a call holds its keys, so a member working together with the server could fake who sent a piece of voice or video. Outsiders and the server alone can’t. Text is signed by each device.

  • Search and threads

    There is no message search, and replies don’t open threads.

  • Checking a contact

    No guided step yet to compare keys with someone in person.

  • Tested on real desktops

    Screen sharing on Linux, Windows and macOS has so far been checked in test runs without a real desktop. The checks on real desktops are still under way.

  • Signed installers

    The Windows setup isn’t code-signed and the Mac app isn’t notarized, so both systems warn on first start. How to open it anyway
